## Main Problem Statement

In recent times, many students face difficulty in identifying the most suitable domain for their career based on their interests, skills, and academic background. Even after selecting a domain, students often remain confused about how to begin their preparation, what skills are required, and the types of companies they should target. This lack of structured guidance leads to inefficient preparation, anxiety, and poor decision-making during campus placements.

Additionally, students struggle to understand how well their resumes align with their chosen domain and job roles. Many are unaware of their skill gaps, resume weaknesses, and readiness level for interviews. Without personalized feedback and adaptive learning support, students find it difficult to improve their preparation strategy effectively.

This problem calls for the development of an intelligent, student-centric guidance system that helps students identify suitable career domains, analyze resumes, understand skill requirements, receive personalized preparation roadmaps, and adapt learning paths based on individual progress. The goal is to reduce confusion, improve preparation efficiency, and enable students to approach placements with clarity and confidence.

# 🟢 EASY LEVEL PROBLEMS (S1-S5)

## S1 - Career Domain Identification
**Problem**: Students cannot easily connect their personal interests and academic background to potential career fields, leaving them without clear direction.

**Example**: A Computer Science student who enjoys graphic design and story writing does not know these interests align perfectly with a career in UX/UI Design. They default to applying only for generic software developer roles.

---

## S2 - Preparation Roadmap Generator
**Problem**: Once a domain is chosen, students lack a basic checklist of initial actions, essential resources, and a timeline to start their preparation effectively.

**Example**: A student decides to pursue "Data Science." They spend weeks overwhelmed, unsure if they should first learn Python, watch tutorials, enroll in a course, or find datasets to practice with, leading to procrastination.

---

## S3 - Automated Resume Review ⭐ (SELECTED)
**Problem**: Students have no simple way to get a quick, automated review of their resume's formatting, clarity, and basic keyword alignment with their target roles.

**Example**: A student applies for "Marketing Analyst" roles but their resume is densely formatted, lacks quantifiable achievements, and misses key terms like "Google Analytics" or "ROI analysis," causing it to be rejected by automated tracking systems (ATS).

---

## S4 - Company Discovery by Domain
**Problem**: There is a lack of centralized, role-specific information on the different types of companies (startups, MNCs, etc.) that hire for a student's chosen field.

**Example**: An Electrical Engineering student targeting "Embedded Systems" roles only knows to apply to large electronics manufacturers. They are unaware of exciting opportunities in automotive companies (e.g., Tesla), aerospace (ISRO), or IoT startups.

---

## S5 - Preparation Level Benchmark
**Problem**: Students cannot benchmark their current preparation level against typical requirements for their target roles to gauge their starting point.

**Example**: A final-year student wants a job in "Consulting." They don't know if their level of case practice, guesstimate skills, and business awareness is at a "beginner," "intermediate," or "ready-for-interview" stage compared to peers.

---

# 🟡 MEDIUM LEVEL PROBLEMS (M1-M3)

## M1 - Skill Prioritization System
**Problem**: Students struggle to prioritize which specific technical and soft skills to develop from a vast list, leading to scattered and shallow learning.

**Example**: For "Product Management," a student finds recommendations for skills like SQL, A/B testing, PRD writing, stakeholder management, and roadmap prioritization. They jump randomly between online articles on each topic without mastering any, leaving them unprepared for in-depth interview questions.

---

## M2 - Adaptive Learning Path ⭐ (SELECTED)
**Problem**: Existing guidance is generic and does not adapt to a student's unique learning pace, strengths, and weaknesses, making study plans inefficient.

**Example**: Two students follow the same "8-week coding interview prep" plan. Student A, who is weak in Data Structures but strong in Algorithms, wastes time on algorithm basics. Student B, with the opposite profile, struggles because the plan doesn't provide extra data structure resources when they fail a practice test.

---

## M3 - Project-Based Learning Planner
**Problem**: It is difficult for students to translate abstract job descriptions into a concrete, project-based learning plan that builds a demonstrable portfolio.

**Example**: A job description for a "Cybersecurity Analyst" requires "understanding of network vulnerabilities" and "incident response." A student doesn't know how to turn this into a hands-on project, like setting up a safe lab environment to simulate an attack or analyzing sample packet captures.

---

# 🔴 HARD LEVEL PROBLEMS (H1-H2)

## H1 - AI-Driven Job Market Mapping ⭐ (SELECTED)
**Problem**: The problem requires an AI system that can dynamically map a student's evolving skill profile against real-time job market trends to recommend the highest-value learning interventions.

**Example**: In early 2023, a student is preparing for cloud roles. The system analyzes real-time job postings and detects a sharp 40% increase in demand for "Azure DevOps" skills compared to "AWS Solutions Architect." It dynamically alerts the student and suggests pivoting a portion of their study plan to include Azure-specific pipelines and infrastructure-as-code.

---

## H2 - Closed-Loop Guidance System
**Problem**: The core challenge is creating a closed-loop guidance system that uses continuous feedback from mock interviews and project assessments to autonomously adjust a student's entire preparation strategy and goals.

**Example**: A student aiming for "Machine Learning Engineer" roles does well on coding tests but consistently receives poor feedback on explaining their project's model trade-offs during mock interviews. The system doesn't just note this weakness; it automatically reduces the focus on new coding problems and injects a new module on "model interpretability and communication," while even suggesting they refine a specific project from their portfolio to better showcase this skill.
